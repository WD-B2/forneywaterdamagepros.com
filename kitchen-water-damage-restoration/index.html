Kitchen water damage: where it hides

Kitchens are full of water lines and drains, so leaks are common. The usual culprits are the sink supply lines and drain, the dishwasher, the refrigerator ice maker line and the faucet. Kitchen water damage is the wetting of cabinets, floors, walls and the cavity behind them by these sources.

Water usually starts under the sink or behind an appliance and travels along the cabinet base and into the floor. By the time a stain shows on the front, the back and bottom of the cabinet may have been wet for weeks.

Signs of a hidden kitchen leak

Check under the sink and behind the dishwasher first.

  • Cabinet bases or toe kicks that swell, bubble or peel.
  • A musty smell inside the sink cabinet.
  • Floors that cup, lift or feel soft near the sink or dishwasher.
  • Water stains on the ceiling below, if the kitchen is upstairs.
  • A water bill that rises with no change in habits.

A flashlight and a paper towel under the pipes will show a slow drip in a minute.

Safety steps while you wait for help

  • Close the shutoff valve under the sink, or the main valve if the leak is bigger.
  • Keep clear of wet outlets and appliances, and turn off the kitchen circuits if you can reach the panel dry.
  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside.
  • Move food and small appliances off the wet counters and floor.

Why kitchens dry slowly in Texas

The Texas State Historical Association describes the climate of Kaufman County as subtropical-humid. Humid air slows drying, and most kitchen cabinets are made of particleboard, which swells when it absorbs water and does not shrink back. Solid wood cabinets fare better, but still need drying.

Because of that, the first 24 to 48 hours matter. The EPA says to dry water-damaged areas within that window to prevent mold. A kitchen with a closed cabinet base, a dishwasher and a floor system on top of it needs deliberate drying, and a fan blowing into the room is usually not enough.

A kitchen job follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, so water is classified first and drying continues until readings reach the dry target.

How kitchen drying works

The goal is to dry behind and under everything.

  1. Stop the leak

    Fix or shut off the source.

  2. Open the space

    Toe kicks come off and cabinet backs may be opened to let air in.

  3. Check wet areas

    Moisture meters map the cabinet, floor and wall.

  4. Dry

    Air movers and a dehumidifier run for several days.

  5. Repair

    Cabinets, flooring and paint are replaced as needed.

First steps when you find a wet cabinet

Shut off the water to the fixture first. Every sink has small stop valves under it, and every dishwasher has one under the sink or behind the unit. Take everything out of the cabinet, dry the surface with towels and leave the door open so air can circulate.

Photograph the damage, then look under the cabinet and along the wall for water that traveled. Do not run the dishwasher again until you know where the leak is, and do not seal a damp cabinet with caulk or paint. If the wood is soft or the base is swollen, call before the damage reaches the floor system.

How to keep the next kitchen leak small

Replace old braided or rubber supply lines and check the drain connections each year. Keep the cabinet under the sink clear enough that you can see a drip. A small tray or a leak sensor under the sink and behind the dishwasher gives an early warning for very little money.

What changes the cost of kitchen water damage

Kitchens cost more because of cabinets.

  • Cabinet materialParticleboard often needs replacement and solid wood may not.
  • Floor typeTile is simple. Wood and laminate are not.
  • DurationSlow leaks soak more.
  • AccessAppliances and countertops may need moving.
  • RepairsCabinets, flooring and paint are separate.
