Water heater emergency: stop the supply first

A leaking water heater is an emergency because the tank refills from the supply line as fast as it drains. Our 24/7 line is open, and while you wait you should close the cold water valve on top of the tank, switch off the power or set a gas unit to pilot, and keep out of any water that has spread toward outlets.

Do not touch a unit that is sparking.

Water heater leak cleanup is the removal of water from the closet, garage or attic where the tank sits, followed by drying of the floor, walls and anything the water reached. The big cases are tank failures. The slow cases are leaking fittings and relief valves that wet a floor for weeks.

Where the tank sits changes the damage. A heater in a garage usually spills onto concrete, which dries well but spreads water toward the house. A heater in an attic can soak insulation and a ceiling below. A heater in an interior closet pushes water into carpet, baseboards and the wall behind it, which is the case most likely to hide moisture.

Signs a tank is failing before it floods

Most tanks warn you. These are the signs worth acting on.

  • Rust-colored water or a metallic taste from the hot tap.
  • Dampness or rust streaks around the base of the tank or the fittings.
  • A rumbling or popping sound from sediment in the tank.
  • Water around the relief valve discharge pipe.
  • A tank that has run for many years with no inspection.

Any one of these deserves a plumber's visit. If the floor is already wet, call us to handle the water while the plumber handles the heater.

How the cleanup works

A water heater loss is small in volume and big in hidden moisture.

  1. Confirm the source is stopped

    The supply valve is closed, power or gas is off, and a plumber is lined up for the repair.

  2. Extract

    Wet vacuums and extraction wands remove water from the floor, carpet and the area behind the tank.

  3. Open and inspect

    Baseboards and the lower wall are checked with moisture meters. A small cut in drywall may be needed to dry the cavity.

  4. Dry

    Air movers and a dehumidifier run until readings return to normal.

  5. Repair

    Trim, drywall and flooring are replaced where needed.

Where the water goes next

Water from a heater closet often reaches nearby rooms. Carpet that touched it needs the steps on the carpet water damage page, and a tank in an attic can lead to a ceiling repair. If the tank sat in a utility closet near the kitchen, the area may overlap with an appliance leak.

If you live in a house on a slab, water tends to run to the lowest edge of the room and under baseboards instead of staying where it fell. That is why we check the wall base a foot or two past the visible wet area. Cleanup after a tank failure follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, starting with extraction and moving to measured drying.

What changes the cost of water heater leak cleanup

A single tank rarely costs the same twice.

  • Volume and durationA burst tank is a flood. A slow leak is longer but smaller.
  • LocationGarage concrete dries easily. Closets and attics need more work.
  • MaterialsCarpet, wood and drywall are more costly than tile and concrete.
  • Hidden moistureWet baseboards and wall bases may need removal and drying.
  • RepairsDrywall, trim and flooring are separate from mitigation.
