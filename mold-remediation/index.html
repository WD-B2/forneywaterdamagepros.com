Musty smell, dark spots or a damp wall: is it mold?

A musty smell, dark or fuzzy spots on drywall, bubbling paint or a ceiling stain that keeps returning are the usual reasons people search for mold help. These signs mean there is moisture in the wrong place. Mold cannot grow without it, so finding the water is as important as removing the growth.

Mold remediation is the controlled removal or cleaning of mold-damaged materials and the correction of the moisture that allowed it to grow. In the industry it follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard, which describes containment, removal, cleaning and verification. We do not diagnose symptoms or make health claims. Questions about how mold affects a person belong to a physician.

Mold needs moisture, and after water damage it often shows up

The EPA says the key to mold control is moisture control, and that wet areas and items should be dried within 24 to 48 hours. The Texas State Historical Association describes Kaufman County's climate as subtropical-humid, so indoor humidity is already a factor here. Add a leak, and drying quickly matters even more.

If you have wet materials, structural drying is what prevents mold. If mold has already appeared, the work moves to removal and then to correcting the water source. Skipping either step leads to trouble. Cleaning mold without fixing the moisture leaves the conditions in place.

Texas rules to know before you hire

The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation says a license is required for mold assessment and mold remediation under Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1958. There are exemptions, including projects of less than 25 contiguous square feet and residential property owners or staff in buildings of 10 or fewer units working on their own property.

State law also says a license holder may not do both the assessment and the remediation on the same project, which keeps the person who finds the problem separate from the person who is paid to fix it. Before work starts, ask for the license number and check it on the TDLR license search at tdlr.texas.gov. We recommend you verify any company the same way, including ours.

How a mold job usually runs

This is a general outline. Scope depends on the size and cause.

  1. Fix the moisture

    A plumber, roofer or HVAC technician stops the water, and drying begins.

  2. Assess

    An assessor, separate from the remediator, inspects and writes a protocol if the job requires one.

  3. Contain

    Plastic barriers and negative air keep spores from spreading.

  4. Remove and clean

    Mold-damaged porous materials are removed and bagged, and hard surfaces are cleaned.

  5. Dry and verify

    The area is dried and checked before rebuild.

What not to do

These mistakes are common and avoidable.

  • Do not paint or caulk over mold. The EPA says paint over mold is likely to peel.
  • Do not use a fan to blow mold across the room.
  • Do not burn or bleach your way out of the problem without fixing the moisture.
  • Do not remove large areas without containment.
  • Do not skip the moisture fix. Mold returns if the water does.

We never promise mold will not return, because that depends on moisture control after the work.
