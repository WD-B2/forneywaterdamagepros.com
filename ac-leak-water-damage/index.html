Where AC leak water comes from

An air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air as it cools, and that water is meant to drain away through a condensate line. When the line clogs, the pan cracks or the coil freezes and thaws, water ends up in ceilings, closets and walls. Turn the system off at the thermostat when you see a leak, because running it keeps adding water.

AC leak water damage is the wetting of drywall, insulation, framing and flooring by condensate. It is usually clean water at first, but it grows slime and algae in drain lines and can turn musty if it sits.

In Forney, air conditioners work long hours. NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals show about 20 days a year at 100 degrees or more at DFW, and cooling systems run most of the summer. That means small clogs matter, because a drain line that backs up on a hot day can overflow a pan in an attic or closet within hours.

Signs your AC is leaking

Some signs show up before the stain.

  • A water stain on the ceiling or wall near an air handler or vent.
  • Water in the pan under the indoor unit, or on the floor of a closet.
  • A musty smell near the return air grille.
  • Ice on the coil or lines, followed by dripping after it melts.
  • The unit shutting off by itself, which can mean a float switch is working.

Any of these deserves a visit from an HVAC technician. If water has already reached a ceiling, see the ceiling water damage page.

Safety steps while you wait for help

  • Turn the system off at the thermostat so it stops making more water.
  • Keep people and furniture out from under a sagging or bulging ceiling.
  • Stay away from light fixtures and outlets in the wet area, and turn off that circuit at the panel if you can reach it dry.
  • Place a bucket under active drips and photograph the damage before cleanup.

How AC leak damage is dried and repaired

The air conditioner is fixed by an HVAC technician. The water is our job.

  1. Turn the unit off

    Stop the unit and confirm the drain line is cleared by a technician.

  2. Find the wet area

    Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far the water traveled.

  3. Remove wet insulation

    Wet insulation holds water and is usually replaced.

  4. Dry

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry framing and drywall.

  5. Repair

    Drywall, texture and paint are restored after readings are normal.

Closets, attics and walls

Many air handlers sit in hall closets or attics. Closet leaks wet carpet and the base of walls, which is the same pattern covered on the wall and drywall page. Attic leaks soak insulation and ceilings below. Left alone, a damp closet can turn into mold remediation, especially in a warm, still space.

The EPA recommends keeping indoor humidity between 30 and 60 percent to limit mold growth, and says wet building materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours. Crews that work to the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard measure moisture in the ceiling, insulation and framing before deciding what stays.

Maintenance that prevents most condensate leaks

Most condensate overflows trace back to a drain line that nobody has cleaned. Algae and dust build up in the line over a cooling season and finally block it. Having an HVAC technician clear the line and check the pan before summer is the most useful step you can take.

A float switch in the pan can shut the system off before water overflows, but it only helps if it is installed and working. Change filters on schedule, because a dirty filter lowers airflow and can freeze the coil. If the system cycles off by itself and the pan is full, do not reset it repeatedly. Call a technician.

What changes the cost of AC leak repair

Condensate water is clean, which keeps many jobs modest, but there are limits.

  • LocationA closet is easier than an attic or second-floor ceiling.
  • DurationWeeks of dripping soak more than hours.
  • InsulationWet insulation is usually replaced.
  • Ceiling or wall areaLarger stains mean more drywall and finish work.
  • MoldA long-damp area may need separate assessment.
