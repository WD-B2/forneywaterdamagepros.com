Appliance leak emergency: shut the valve and unplug

An appliance leak is urgent because it runs until someone stops it, often while nobody is home. Our 24/7 line is open, and while you wait you should close the small supply valve behind the machine, unplug it if the cord is dry, and keep walking out of the wet area. If the valve is stuck, shut the home's main water valve.

Appliance leak water damage is the wetting of floors, cabinets and walls from washing machines, dishwashers, refrigerator ice maker lines and similar equipment. The Texas Department of Insurance names a washer hose break as a typical sudden and accidental loss, which is why these claims are common.

The damage usually starts at a hose or a seal. Rubber supply hoses fail at the connection or burst under pressure. Dishwasher seals and drain hoses leak a little with every cycle.

Ice maker lines are thin and run behind refrigerators where leaks go unseen. Each case wets a different part of the house.

Knowing the likely failure helps you find the water.

Safety steps while you wait for help

  • Close the supply valve to the appliance, or the main valve if you cannot find it.
  • Unplug the appliance only if the outlet and the floor around it are dry.
  • Do not use the appliance again until the cause is found.
  • Lift rugs and belongings off the wet floor and photograph everything first.

Signs of a slow appliance leak

Slow leaks cause more damage than floods because they run longer unseen.

  • A floor that feels soft or looks warped near the appliance.
  • A musty smell in the laundry room or kitchen.
  • Baseboards that swell or paint that peels beside the machine.
  • Cabinet bases that stain or feel damp inside.
  • A higher water bill with no change in use.

Pull the appliance away from the wall and look behind and under it. A dry floor under a leaking hose is rare.

Cleaning up after an appliance leak

Water from an appliance is usually clean, which makes fast extraction effective. Hard floors dry well. Wet carpet and pad are handled as described on the carpet page, and laminate or wood needs the careful steps on the hardwood floor page. Where the water reached the cabinet, the kitchen water damage page explains toe kicks and cabinet bases.

If a leak ran under a floor or inside a wall for weeks, expect more than surface work. Moisture readings decide how much needs to be opened. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, and slow leaks that are older than that may already involve mold remediation.

The drying plan follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which ties equipment and timing to what the cabinets, floor and subfloor actually measure.

How to prevent the next appliance leak

Most appliance floods start with a rubber hose that has aged out. Replacing old supply hoses with braided stainless steel versions costs little compared with a soaked floor. Look at every connection once a year for bulges, rust or white mineral crust, which signal a slow leak.

Make sure you know where each appliance shutoff valve is and that it turns. A valve that has never been moved can seize. A drain pan under the washer or water heater gives a leak somewhere to go besides the floor. Turn the washer's supply valves off when you leave for a trip, and you remove the biggest risk of coming home to a flood.

What changes the cost of an appliance leak

It depends on how long the leak ran and what it soaked.

  • DurationA leak caught in an hour is small. One that ran for days is not.
  • Floor typeTile dries fast. Laminate, wood and carpet need more care or replacement.
  • Cabinet involvementWet cabinet bases may need removal and drying.
  • Subfloor and wall basesHidden moisture adds equipment days.
  • RepairsFlooring, baseboards and cabinets are separate from drying.
