Hardwood floor water damage: what happens to wood

Wood is a sponge that swells across the grain when it absorbs water. A floor that was flat on Monday can cup by Thursday, with the edges higher than the center, or crown if it dries unevenly. Hardwood floor water damage is that swelling, plus staining, finish failure and, if the water lasts, rot and mold underneath.

Not every wood floor reacts the same way. Solid hardwood, engineered wood and laminate behave differently. Solid boards can often be dried and refinished.

Engineered floors have a thin top layer glued over plywood or fiberboard and are harder to save. Laminate has a fiberboard core that swells and does not return to shape.

How wood floors are dried

The goal is to let moisture out slowly so the wood does not crack.

  1. Extract and inspect

    Surface water is removed and the floor is checked with a moisture meter.

  2. Open the system

    A few boards or baseboards may be removed so air can reach the subfloor.

  3. Dry with controlled airflow

    Floor drying mats, air movers and a dehumidifier pull moisture out of the boards and subfloor.

  4. Monitor daily

    Readings are compared with dry reference wood until they match.

  5. Wait and assess

    Cupped boards often relax as they dry. Sanding and refinishing come only after moisture is normal.

Why timing matters on a slab

Many Forney homes are built on a slab, and wood laid over concrete has no airflow underneath. Water that gets between the two has nowhere to go except up through the wood. The Texas State Historical Association describes Kaufman County's climate as subtropical-humid, and humid air slows drying even further.

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours. For a wood floor, that is not a suggestion. Starting early is the difference between a floor that can be saved and one that has to come up.

Waiting for it to dry on its own rarely works. Drying a wood floor follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and the readings, not the surface, decide when it is done.

Signs the floor needs attention

Do not wait for the floor to buckle.

  • Edges of boards higher than the center, which is cupping.
  • Center of boards higher than edges, which is crowning.
  • Gaps between boards after a stretch of wetness, or boards that lift away from the subfloor.
  • White or dark stains in the finish.
  • A musty smell near the floor, especially along walls.

Do not sand or refinish a floor that has not been checked with a moisture meter.

When the floor cannot be saved

Wood that has been soaked for days, or that lies over a continuing leak, may need replacement. In that case the work resembles structural drying of the subfloor first and then new flooring. If the water came from underneath, see the slab leak page, and for leaks from the kitchen, see the kitchen water damage page. Mold can grow under wet flooring, and mold remediation explains the next steps.

What to do while you wait for help

Wipe up surface water with towels and move rugs and furniture off the floor, using felt pads or foil under legs that must stay. Do not use a heat gun or a space heater to speed things up. Fast heat dries the top of the board before the core and can crack it.

Keep the air conditioning running to hold humidity down, and open interior doors so air can reach the wet area. Photograph the floor from several angles with the date visible, and write down when the leak started. If the water came from a leak, shut it off first. A floor that keeps getting wet cannot be dried.
