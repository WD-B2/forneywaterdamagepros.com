Where Historic Downtown sits and what is in it

Historic Downtown Forney is the older center of the city, around Main Street and Bois d'Arc Street. The City of Forney describes it as the heart of the community, and the streets in and around it include Main, Broad, Bois d'Arc, McGraw, Pacific and Trinity.

Landmarks make it easy to place a call. City Hall is at 101 E. Main Street, the Spellman Museum of Forney History is at 200 S.

Bois d'Arc Street, and Hamblen Park sits behind City Hall. Tell us the nearest one and we can usually picture where you are.

Older houses beside newer ones

Forney grew up around the Texas and Pacific Railroad, which reached the town in 1873. The Texas Historical Commission marks the Dick P. Moore House at 306 S.

Bois d'Arc Street, built in 1910, and a local news report dates the Potts-Reagin House at 315 S. Bois d'Arc to 1895.

Houses of that age stand among newer ones, so the plumbing, wiring and roofing on one street can come from different decades. We do not assume what you have. A plumber or a moisture reading will show what is behind your wall, and age alone does not tell us how much water damage a house will see.

If water is spreading in an older building

Work through these in order and stop if anything feels unsafe.

Find the shutoff

The main valve may be at the street meter, in a crawl space or inside a utility closet. Close it if you can reach it safely.

Keep power and water apart

Stay out of standing water if outlets, cords or appliances are wet. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical danger.

Lift what you can

Raise rugs, boxes, books and electronics. Leave heavy antiques where they stand and tell us about them when we call.

Mind the ceiling

A bulging or sagging ceiling can hold a lot of water. Keep people out from under it.

Keep dirty water away from people

Water from a drain, a toilet or the street should be treated as contaminated. Keep children and pets out.

Photograph first

Take photos and a short video before anything is moved. Your insurer will ask for them.

How extraction and drying work in an older building

A crew starts by finding the source and deciding what kind of water it is. Clean water from a supply line is handled differently from water that came up through a drain. Then the standing water is pulled out, and the structure is dried with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Drying is measured, not guessed. Moisture meters read the walls, floors and framing, and the readings are repeated until they match dry material. Tell the crew about original trim, plaster, hardwood or anything you want kept. Some pieces can be saved and others cannot, and the readings help decide which.

Crews that work to the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard classify the water first and keep drying until readings match dry targets. The water extraction page and the structural drying page explain the equipment and the timing.

Documenting the loss for your insurer

Photos taken before cleanup help show what the damage looked like. Moisture readings and an itemized scope can support the record of what was done. Keep receipts for anything you buy to limit the damage.

Whether the loss is covered depends on the cause and on your policy, so read it and ask your insurer. If you rent or run a shop, tell your landlord or property manager at the same time. We do not decide coverage, and we cannot promise what an insurer will pay.
