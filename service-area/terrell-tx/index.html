Around Terrell: the railroad town on US 80

Terrell is a Kaufman County city of about 17,500 people, and US 80 runs through the center of town. It was founded in 1873 as a Texas and Pacific Railroad town, and it still has the grid of older streets that comes with that history.

Interstate 20 lies on the south side and State Highway 34 on the east, which is why most calls reach us from either the US 80 side of town or the neighborhoods near I-20.

Landmarks help us place a call fast. The Round House, an octagonal house, Terrell State Hospital, Ben Gill Park, where the Terrell Jubilee is held, and the municipal airport with its British Flying Training School Museum are all easy to find. If you give us a street near one of them, we can usually tell which side of town you are on in a sentence.

Many leaks are found by someone walking in the door after a long day. If that happened to you, take photos before anything is moved and call the line, because a leak that ran for hours has usually reached more than the room you can see.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading in Terrell

Do these in order and stop if anything feels unsafe.

Close the main valve

Turn it clockwise at the house or at the meter box. Open a low faucet to drain the line.

Power down wet rooms

Flip breakers for wet rooms if the panel is dry. If you cannot reach it safely, call the utility or 911.

Lift the small things

Move shoes, rugs, laptops and papers up. Leave heavy furniture where it stands.

Keep people out of dirty water

If water came from a toilet, a sewer line or the outdoors, keep children and pets away.

Photograph before anything moves

Take photos and a short video with the date visible. Your insurer will want them.

Older home or newer build: what changes in Terrell

A town founded in 1873 has houses of many ages. If yours is decades old, the supply lines, shutoff valves and drain lines may be original or partly replaced, and valves that have not moved in years can seize when you need them.

Test the main valve once a year so that on the day it matters, it turns. If your home is newer, the risk shifts to appliance hoses, water heater fittings and slab-level plumbing, which a plumber and a drying crew handle together.

In both cases we start with the same question: where did the water come from? A pipe leak is handled as described on the burst pipe page. A wet ceiling points to the ceiling water damage page. Water from outside, after the kind of storm that crosses Kaufman County, belongs to flood damage cleanup.
