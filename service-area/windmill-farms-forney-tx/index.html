A big community with newer houses

Windmill Farms is a master-planned community in Forney, ZIP code 75126. Listing sites describe it as covering close to 4,000 acres, with about 5.5 miles of trails, 11 ponds and two community pools.

Homes were built in phases, and builders such as LGI Homes have opened new sections within the community. Listing sources disagree about when building began, so we describe the homes as newer construction from the 2000s through the 2020s.

Why newer homes still have water damage

A newer house avoids some problems and leaves others. Washer hoses, dishwasher lines, ice maker lines, water heater fittings and air conditioner drain pans fail at any age, and the cause is usually a part that wore out or was installed loosely.

Many of these leaks start small and run for hours. A supply line fails while the family is at work or asleep, and the first sign is a soft floor or a stain on the ceiling below. The appliance leak page and the AC leak page cover those cases.

What a visit looks like in a Windmill Farms home

Every job differs, but the sequence is usually the same.

  1. The phone call

    You describe the source, the rooms and whether anything electrical is wet. We tell you what to do while help is lined up.

  2. The source

    The leak is stopped or isolated. A plumber or appliance repair comes separately if a part must be replaced.

  3. Extraction and readings

    Standing water is removed, and meters map how far the moisture went into walls, cabinets and floors.

  4. Drying

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run while the readings are repeated. Equipment moves as the numbers change.

  5. Repair planning

    Drywall, flooring and paint are restored only after the readings show dry material.

Ponds, greenbelts and heavy rain

Listing descriptions mention homes that back onto ponds and greenbelts. That does not mean a home floods. We have no evidence that Windmill Farms has a history of flooding, and we do not claim one.

If you live on a lot that slopes toward the house, heavy rain is a good reason to look at where the water goes. Clear gutters and keep soil sloped away from the foundation. If rain does get inside, the flood damage cleanup page explains why that water is handled as contaminated.
