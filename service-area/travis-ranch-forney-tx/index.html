Where Travis Ranch is

Travis Ranch is a master-planned community in Forney, ZIP code 75126, at the southern tip of Lake Ray Hubbard. Listing sites put it at about 1,500 acres, and county plat names show it was built in phases such as Phase 2E, 2I and 3A.

Lewis Elementary in Forney ISD sits within the community, according to a neighborhood directory, and the community has pools, trails and a splash pad. Homes there date from about 2005 to 2023, so it is a mix of older and newer construction.

Lakeside is not the same as flood-prone

Living near Lake Ray Hubbard sounds like a flood story, but we have no evidence of a flooding history in Travis Ranch and we do not claim one. Most water damage in homes like these starts indoors.

That is why this page is about plumbing, appliances and air conditioners first. If heavy rain does push water into a home, the flood damage cleanup page explains how that water is handled.

Upstairs leaks reach the rooms below

In a two-story house, a leak from a second-floor bathroom, laundry room or air handler drips into the ceiling below. The first sign is often a brown ring on the downstairs ceiling, and by then the insulation above it is soaked.

A crew checks the ceiling, the wall cavities and the floor above with a moisture meter before anything is cut. The ceiling water damage page explains when a stain can be dried in place and when the drywall has to come out.

Before anyone arrives

Do these in order and stop if anything feels unsafe.

Stop the source

Close the fixture shutoff or the main valve. For an air conditioner leak, turn the system off at the thermostat.

Protect the people

Keep everyone out from under a sagging ceiling. Do not use light fixtures that are wet or dripping.

Catch and lift

Place containers under drips and move furniture and electronics away from the wet area.

Avoid dirty water

Water from a toilet or drain may carry bacteria. Keep children and pets away and do not use a household vacuum on it.

Record it

Take photos and video of the ceiling, the floor and the source before cleanup.

Drying and documentation

After standing water is removed, air movers and dehumidifiers pull moisture from the framing and drywall. Readings are repeated each day and equipment is moved as they change. Work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard when a crew uses it, so ask whichever company you hire whether its crews do.

For your insurer, photos, readings and an itemized scope help describe the loss. Coverage depends on the cause and your policy, and we cannot promise what an insurer will pay.
