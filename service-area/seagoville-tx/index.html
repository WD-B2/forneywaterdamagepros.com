What seasons mean for water damage in Seagoville

Seagoville sits at about 440 feet along US 175 in Dallas County, with a small piece reaching into Kaufman County. Seasons set the pattern of calls. Spring and fall storms bring heavy rain and runoff.

Summer heat keeps air conditioners running, and clogged drain lines overflow. Winter freezes, rare but real, split pipes in attics and exterior walls.

The Texas State Historical Association lists Kaufman County rainfall at about 39 inches a year, and the National Weather Service recorded a low of minus 2 degrees at DFW on February 16, 2021. Both extremes affect the roughly 18,400 people of Seagoville, and both show up as water in the house.

When water is spreading in Seagoville

If water is moving through a house, close the main valve, switch off breakers for wet rooms if the panel is dry and call the line. Seagoville Public Works lists 972-287-6823, and the city's water and sewer number is 972-287-2050. Call them for a main break or street-side leak and call us for the cleanup.

Where the city's line ends and yours begins

Seagoville buys its water from Dallas Water Utilities, and a city document explains the boundary clearly. The city is responsible for the line up to the meter, and the customer is responsible from the meter to the home.

That boundary matters for a leak you cannot see. A wet patch in the yard between the meter and the house, a spike in the water bill or a warm spot on the floor points to your side.

Those symptoms often mean a leak in a line under the slab or in the yard, covered on the slab leak page. A plumber finds and repairs the leak. We dry the floor, walls and anything the water reached, working with the plumber's schedule.

Before we get there: if water is still spreading in Seagoville

Take these steps in order.

Shut the water off

The main valve at the house, or the meter shutoff if the valve is stuck.

Switch off the power to wet rooms

Only if the panel is dry. Otherwise call the utility or 911.

Lift what you can

Move small items and electronics up, and keep wet rugs off wood.
