Water damage restoration in Forney

Water damage restoration and repair for Forney homes and businesses

Water damage restoration is the process of removing water from a building, drying the structure and repairing what the water harmed. In Forney it usually starts with a phone call about a burst pipe, a failed water heater, a roof leak or rain that came in under a door. The earlier water comes out, the fewer materials have to be replaced.

We take emergency calls 24/7 from Forney, Terrell, Mesquite, Seagoville and the towns along US 80. A licensed and insured crew is lined up as availability allows, and we do not promise arrival times because they depend on the night you call. What we do promise is plain answers about what to do first and a scope and price explained before work starts.

The work follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. That means classifying the water, extracting it, drying with air movers and dehumidifiers, and checking moisture readings before anything is closed up or rebuilt. Water damage repair, the drywall, flooring and paint, comes after the building is dry, not before.

Where water damage in Forney comes from

Forney is a city of about 35,000 people on US 80 in northwestern Kaufman County, roughly 20 miles from downtown Dallas. The Texas State Historical Association describes the county's soils as clayey and puts rainfall at about 39 inches a year. Clay drains slowly, so after heavy rain the ground around a house can stay saturated and push water toward a garage or slab.

Cold is the other source. The National Weather Service in Fort Worth recorded a low of minus 2 degrees at DFW on February 16, 2021, and NOAA's records tie that freeze to burst pipes across Texas. Homes here are built for mild winters, so attics and exterior walls are where pipes split first. The frozen pipe page and the burst pipe page explain what to do.

Wind is a third. An EF-3 tornado struck near Forney on April 3, 2012, with preliminary winds up to 150 miles per hour, and the worst damage was in the Diamond Creek subdivision. Crosby Elementary also lost part of its roof. A roof opened by wind lets rain into attics and ceilings, which is the case on the storm water damage page.

Water damage repair after the drying is done

Repair is the last stage, not the first. Once readings show the framing and subfloor are dry, drywall is replaced, flooring is repaired or installed and walls are painted. Rebuilding on damp materials is how stains return and mold appears, so the order matters. Structural drying is the stage that decides how much repair is needed.

Some floors and walls can be saved. Others cannot. The hardwood floor page, the carpet page and the wall and drywall page explain how those decisions are made, and the EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours, which is why we ask you to call quickly.

Insurance questions after water damage

Many Texas homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or a washer hose that fails, and often do not cover slow leaks or flood water. The Texas Department of Insurance says flood needs a separate policy. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and take photos before anything is moved. The TDI help line is 1-800-252-3439.
