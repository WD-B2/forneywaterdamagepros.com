Can wet carpet be saved?

Often yes, when the water was clean and the carpet was wet for less than a day or two. Carpet fibers dry quickly, but the pad underneath holds water like a sponge, and the subfloor below it stays wet longer. Carpet water damage restoration is the extraction and drying of carpet, pad and subfloor, or removal and replacement of what cannot be saved.

The question that decides everything is the water category. Clean water from a supply line is far more forgiving than gray or black water. Carpet that touched sewage or flood water is almost always removed, because it cannot be reliably sanitized.

How wet carpet is dried

A good job treats the carpet, the pad and the floor as three layers.

  1. Extract

    Truck-mounted or portable extractors pull water from the carpet and pad.

  2. Lift and separate

    Corners may be lifted so air can move under the carpet, or the pad removed.

  3. Dry

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the carpet, the pad if kept and the subfloor.

  4. Treat

    Antimicrobial treatment can help control odor and bacteria.

  5. Verify and reinstall

    Readings confirm the floor is dry before the carpet is put back or replaced.

Why the pad and the floor matter

The pad is the problem more often than the carpet. It is dense, holds water for days and is cheap to replace compared with the risk of leaving it wet. Under it, a concrete slab can hold moisture for a long time and a wood subfloor can swell. Moisture readings on the floor under the carpet are the only way to know whether it is dry.

The EPA says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Carpet that smells musty after cleanup usually means the pad or floor is still wet, which is why we recommend readings over a smell test. Whether carpet and pad are saved follows the water category defined in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, so the source of the water matters.

Signs carpet is not dry

A dry-looking surface can be deceptive.

  • A musty smell that returns after the room is closed up.
  • Carpet that feels cool or damp when pressed.
  • Dark stains or spots at the edges.
  • Ripples or loose carpet.
  • Floor under the carpet that reads wet on a meter.

Mold in carpet from water damage is a sign the moisture lasted too long. See the mold remediation page if you see it.

What to do while you wait

Pull furniture off wet carpet if you can, and put foil or wood blocks under the legs of anything heavy. Remove loose items and rugs, and blot standing water with towels. Do not walk on the wet area more than you need to, because it pushes water deeper into the pad.

Turn the air conditioner on and run ceiling fans to keep air moving. Avoid a household vacuum, which is not made for water. If the water came from a leak, make sure the leak is stopped. Take photos of the carpet and the stains before anything is moved, since they support an insurance claim.

Why wet carpet smells and how that is handled

A musty odor from carpet usually comes from the pad and the floor below it, not the carpet fibers. Bacteria and mold thrive in damp padding. Drying alone often removes the smell.

If it remains, a crew can treat the carpet and pad with an antimicrobial product or replace the pad. Deodorizer sprays that cover the smell without drying the layers leave the cause in place.
