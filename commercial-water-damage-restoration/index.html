Commercial water emergency: protect the space and the inventory

When water gets into a business, every hour is lost revenue. Our 24/7 line is open, and while you wait you should shut off the water supply if that is the source, move inventory and electronics off the floor, and keep customers and staff out of wet areas. If water is near electrical equipment, leave and call the utility or 911 first.

Commercial water damage restoration covers shops, offices, restaurants, medical suites, warehouses and rental space. It includes water extraction, drying, content handling and coordination with property managers and insurers. The work is similar to residential drying, but the scale, schedule and paperwork differ.

Forney businesses, from downtown storefronts to the US 80 corridor

Forney is a city of about 35,000 people by the Census Bureau's 2023 estimate, and its businesses range from storefronts in Historic Downtown to shopping centers along US 80. The downtown streets, including Main, Broad, Bois D'Arc, McGraw, Pacific and Trinity, hold many small shops and offices, and a leak in one unit can reach a neighboring tenant through a shared wall or ceiling.

That is why we ask about the building when you call. A single-tenant building is a simpler job than a strip center. Shared walls, shared roofs and shared plumbing mean the landlord, the tenant and each insurer may all be involved. Commercial crews work to the same ANSI/IICRC S500 standard as residential ones, with documentation written for managers and insurers.

How a commercial job runs

The plan is built around keeping the business open if possible.

  1. Stop the source and secure the space

    The water is shut off, power is checked and hazards are marked.

  2. Protect inventory

    Stock and equipment are moved or covered. Contents handling may be part of the scope.

  3. Extract and open

    Water is pulled out, wet ceiling tiles and base materials come out where needed.

  4. Dry and monitor

    Equipment runs on a schedule that fits your hours, and readings are logged.

  5. Document and repair

    Records support the claim, and repairs are scheduled to reopen quickly.

What to document for a commercial claim

A good record speeds claims.

  • Photos and video of the damage and the source before cleanup.
  • A list of damaged inventory and equipment.
  • Dates and times when you noticed the problem and shut off the water.
  • Copies of the lease and insurance policies.
  • Moisture readings and the drying log from the restoration crew.

Report the loss to your landlord and insurer as soon as possible.

Keeping a business open during drying

Not every loss closes a business. A leak in the back room can often be contained while the front stays open, with plastic barriers, floor protection and equipment placed to stay out of customer paths. Warning signs, cord covers and a clear entrance help keep customers and staff safe.

Ask for a plan that shows which areas are affected, which are not and how long each stage is expected to take. When the plan changes, the crew should tell you the same day. A written scope and a daily drying log make it easier for your landlord and insurer to follow what is happening.
