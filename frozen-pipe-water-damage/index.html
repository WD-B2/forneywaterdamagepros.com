Emergency after a freeze: the flood often starts at the thaw

Frozen pipe damage is an emergency because the real flooding often starts when the ice melts and the split opens. Our 24/7 line is open through freezes and the thaw that follows. If a pipe is frozen but not leaking, keep the main valve closed until a plumber checks it, and if water is already running, close the valve, open a faucet to relieve pressure and call.

Frozen pipe water damage is the wetting of walls, ceilings, floors and insulation after ice splits a supply line or fitting. The break is usually small, a hairline split or a popped joint, but it can run for hours before anyone sees a stain, especially inside an attic or exterior wall.

North Texas does not freeze often, which is part of the problem. NOAA climate normals for Dallas-Fort Worth show an average of 29 freezes per season, and the Texas State Historical Association lists a January average low of 33 degrees in Kaufman County. Homes here are often built for mild winters, so pipes in attics, garages and exterior walls carry little protection when a hard freeze arrives.

What February 2021 taught Forney homeowners

On February 16, 2021, the National Weather Service in Fort Worth recorded a low of minus 2 degrees at DFW, and the airport's official snowfall for the event was 5.0 inches. NOAA's records tie that freeze to burst water pipes and boil water advisories across the state. Many homes had no leak until power and heat returned and the pipes thawed.

The lesson is practical. After a hard freeze, check every wall and ceiling that backs onto an exterior wall or attic, even if nothing looks wrong, and watch your water meter. If it moves when every fixture is off, a pipe has split somewhere. Drying after a freeze follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, with readings taken until the walls and floors reach their dry targets.

What to do while a pipe is frozen or just thawed

Slow, careful steps keep a bad morning from getting worse.

  1. Close the main valve

    This stops flow before a thawing split opens. Leave it closed until the pipe is checked.

  2. Open the faucets

    Open the ones served by the frozen line so melting water has a place to go.

  3. Look and listen

    Check ceilings, attic hatches and exterior walls for drips, stains or hissing.

  4. Thaw slowly

    Warm air from a hair dryer or space heater placed safely away from water is fine. Never use an open flame.

  5. Call for extraction

    If water has already spread, call the line before the wet materials sit overnight.

Where frozen pipes usually split in Forney homes

Texas homes freeze in predictable places.

  • Supply lines in the attic, where insulation sits on top of the pipe but heat does not reach it.
  • Pipes in exterior walls, especially on the north side of the house.
  • Garage lines, including washer connections and water heater supply lines.
  • Outdoor spigots and the short run of pipe behind them.
  • Sprinkler and irrigation lines, which can send water back toward the foundation.

Water from an attic split usually shows up as a ceiling stain or sag first, and a garage split often reaches the water heater area.

What changes the cost after a freeze

Freeze losses vary widely because the water can travel.

  • Where the split isAn attic split wets ceilings and insulation below. A garage split often stays on concrete.
  • Time it ranMany freeze splits run until someone notices. Longer runs mean more materials.
  • Number of splitsA hard freeze can split several lines at once, each with its own wet area.
  • Materials affectedInsulation, drywall and carpet pad often need replacement, while tile and slab can be dried.
  • RepairsDrywall, paint and flooring are separate from drying and extraction.
