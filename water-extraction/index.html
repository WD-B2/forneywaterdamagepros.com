When standing water is an emergency

Water that sits on a floor for more than a day starts damaging materials you cannot see, so treat any standing water as urgent. Our line is open 24/7, and while you wait you can stop the source, shut off the main water valve and keep everyone out of the water until you know whether it touches a power source.

If you can reach the electrical panel without stepping in water, switch off the breakers for the wet rooms. Move rugs, laptops and boxes to dry ground.

Water extraction is the removal of standing and absorbed water from a building using pumps, wet vacuums and truck-mounted or portable extraction units. It comes first because drying and repair both depend on the water being out. Done early, extraction often decides whether a floor can be saved or has to be replaced.

In Forney the water usually comes from one of two places. Either a line or appliance failed inside the house, or rain arrived faster than the ground could take it. The Texas State Historical Association puts Kaufman County rainfall at about 39 inches a year on clayey subsoils, and clay drains slowly, so a yard can stay saturated after a storm and push water toward a garage or slab.

We ask early whether the water came from inside or outside, because that changes how it is handled.

What a water extraction visit looks like

The order of the work matters more than any single machine.

  1. Find and stop the source

    A closed valve, a stopped appliance or a patched opening comes before any pumping. Otherwise the water refills as fast as it leaves.

  2. Classify the water

    Clean supply water, gray water and contaminated water are treated differently under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and the category decides what can be kept.

  3. Remove the bulk water

    Submersible pumps handle depth. Wands and truck-mounted units pull water from carpet, pad and hard floors, often in several passes.

  4. Check what is left

    Moisture meters and a thermal camera show wet spots under floors and inside walls, which is where the drying plan starts.

Signs water is still hiding after the floor looks dry

A dry surface can sit on a wet structure. These are the usual giveaways.

  • A musty smell that comes back after you air out the room.
  • Baseboards that swell, paint that bubbles or drywall that feels cool and soft.
  • Floors that cup, buckle or feel spongy underfoot.
  • Windows that fog or air that feels heavy even with the air conditioning running.
  • A moisture reading higher than the same material in a dry room, which is the only reliable test.

If you see two or more of these, ask for moisture readings before anyone closes a wall or lays new flooring.

Extraction is the first half of the job

Pulling water out is not the same as drying the building. Walls, subfloors and cabinet bases hold moisture that a wet vacuum cannot reach, so most jobs continue into structural drying with air movers and dehumidifiers. Wet carpet and pad can often be extracted in place, which is covered on the carpet water damage page, while hardwood floors need a slower, more careful approach.

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. When wet materials sit past that window, mold remediation can become part of the job. That deadline is the reason we push for extraction the same day when a crew is available, and why we would rather you call at midnight than wait for morning.

Water category, what it means and what happens next

The S500 standard sorts water into three categories. The category sets the plan, so we ask about the source on the first call.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat it means for the job
Category 1Supply line, tank or faucetClean at first, but it degrades with time. Fast extraction and drying often save flooring and drywall.
Category 2Washer or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow with urine onlyContaminated enough to need cleaning agents. Carpet pad and porous materials are often removed.
Category 3Sewage, rising flood water, long-standing waterTreated as unsafe. Porous materials come out and surfaces are cleaned and sanitized.

Categories describe the water, not the cost. Clean water left for days can move up a category.

What changes the cost of a water extraction job

We do not quote prices on a web page because the water decides the price. These are the factors that move it.

  • Water categoryClean water is the simplest. Contaminated water adds protective gear, disposal and sanitizing.
  • Area and depthA wet kitchen and a flooded garage are different jobs, and total square footage drives labor and equipment.
  • Materials affectedTile and concrete extract easily. Carpet, pad, hardwood and drywall take more work.
  • Time since the lossThe longer water sits, the more it spreads and the more drying it needs afterward.
  • Drying and rebuild scopeExtraction often leads into drying equipment days and then repairs, which are priced separately.
