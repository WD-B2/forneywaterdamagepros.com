Wall water damage: what drywall does when wet

Drywall is gypsum between sheets of paper, and it wicks water upward like a candle wick. A wall that is wet at the bottom can be wet a foot or two above the visible line. Wall and drywall water damage includes swollen baseboards, bubbling paint, soft or sagging drywall, stains and the wet insulation and framing behind them.

Whether a wall can be saved depends on the water and the time. Clean water that is dried within a day or two often leaves drywall intact. Drywall that has been soaked for days, or touched by contaminated water, is usually cut out.

Why many walls in the area got wet in 2021

NOAA's records tie the February 2021 freeze to burst water pipes across Texas, and the National Weather Service in Fort Worth recorded a low of minus 2 degrees at DFW on February 16, 2021. Many pipes in exterior walls split, and the water ran inside the wall cavity until someone found it.

That is the typical pattern for wall damage: the leak is hidden, and the damage shows up on the surface late.

If a pipe has leaked in a wall, drying the cavity matters as much as replacing the drywall. Closing a wall over damp framing and insulation is how mold starts, and the EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours. Deciding between drying and replacing drywall follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which relies on moisture readings and not on appearance.

Safety steps while you wait for help

  • Shut off the water at the source or the main valve.
  • Turn off the breaker for outlets in or near the wet wall before anyone touches them.
  • Do not cut into a wet wall near wiring or plumbing without knowing what runs inside.
  • Move furniture away from the baseboards and photograph the wall.

Signs a wall is wet inside

Surface checks can miss what is behind the paint.

  • Bubbling or peeling paint, especially near the floor.
  • Baseboards that swell or pull away from the wall.
  • Stains that grow or return after drying.
  • Soft drywall that dents when pressed.
  • Musty smell near the wall or a cool spot on a thermal camera.

A moisture meter against the wall gives a number. A thermal camera shows where the water traveled.

How a wet wall is dried and repaired

The repair is last. The drying comes first.

  1. Find the source

    Plumber, roofer or HVAC repair stops the water.

  2. Check the wall

    Meters and thermal imaging show how high the water went.

  3. Open the wall

    A flood cut about two feet above the floor, or a small cutout at the wet area, lets air in.

  4. Dry the cavity

    Insulation comes out if wet and air movers dry the framing.

  5. Replace and finish

    New drywall, tape, texture and paint match the room.

What you can do before the crew arrives

Move furniture away from the wet wall and take pictures of the stain, the baseboard and the floor. Pull the baseboard gently if it is already loose, so air can reach the bottom of the wall. Do not punch holes in drywall unless you have to, because small clean cuts are easier to repair than large ragged ones.

Keep the room cool and the air moving. A fan pointed at the base of the wall helps, as long as the power is safe. Do not paint, caulk or wallpaper over a damp wall. Check the other side of the wall too, since a leak often wets both rooms, and look in closets and behind furniture that is flush to the wall.

Insulation inside the wall

Fiberglass insulation inside an exterior wall holds water against the framing and the back of the drywall. Wet insulation is usually removed because it dries too slowly to protect the wood. Closed-cell foam is different, and a crew will tell you what you have when the wall is opened. The insulation decision is one reason moisture readings matter more than looks.
