Gurgling drains and a sewage smell: is it a backup?

A backup usually announces itself before the water arrives. Drains gurgle, toilets bubble when the washer drains, a sewage smell drifts through a bathroom or utility room, and slow drains appear in several fixtures at once. When wastewater finally comes up through a floor drain, shower or toilet, it is already a contamination event. Treat it that way.

Sewage backup cleanup is the removal of wastewater from a building, the disposal of materials that cannot be cleaned, and the cleaning and sanitizing of everything else. It follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for Category 3 water, which assumes contamination and calls for protective equipment and careful handling.

Sewage emergency: what to do right now

Sewage is an emergency because contaminated water spreads bacteria onto surfaces and into porous materials. Our line is open 24/7. While you wait, keep everyone, including pets, out of the area.

Do not run water, flush toilets or use the washer and dishwasher, because that sends more water into the line. If the water reaches outlets or the panel, call the utility or 911 before you call us.

Do not try to clean up sewage with a wet vac or a mop. A household wet vac sprays contaminated mist into the air and cannot be properly cleaned afterward. Wash hands thoroughly after any contact, and do not touch food that was exposed.

Is the problem in your line or the city's?

The City of Forney provides water and sewer service, and its wastewater is treated through the North Texas Municipal Water District system. In many cities the property owner is responsible for the private line from the house to the connection, and the city handles the main.

If every drain in the house backs up, or if neighbors have the same problem, the cause may be in the main, and the city should be told. If only one fixture backs up, the cause is often a blockage in the house line.

Ask the City of Forney which section of line is yours before you pay for a repair. A plumber can run a camera down the line and show you where the blockage or break is. That evidence also helps with an insurance claim or a request to the city.

How the cleanup is done

Protective gear and containment come first.

  1. Set up protection

    Crews wear gloves, boots, eye protection and respirators, and seal doorways to keep contamination from spreading.

  2. Extract the water

    Pumps and extraction units remove the wastewater into proper disposal.

  3. Remove porous materials

    Wet carpet, pad, drywall and insulation are cut out and bagged.

  4. Clean and sanitize

    Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial product.

  5. Dry and verify

    Equipment dries the structure, then readings confirm it before rebuild.

Related damage after a backup

Sewage often arrives with large volumes of water, so water extraction is the first stage, and a flood of outside water follows the same Category 3 rules on the flood damage page. Once the area is clean, structural drying makes sure the framing is dry. If a backup sat for days, mold remediation may follow.
