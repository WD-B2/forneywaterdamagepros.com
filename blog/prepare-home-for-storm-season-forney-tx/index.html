How to Prepare Your Forney, TX Home for Storm Season

Spring storms hit North Texas hard. A few hours of prep before the season can keep a heavy rain from becoming a water loss.

Call (844) 950-6900

Written by the Forney Water Damage Pros team. Published November 13, 2026.

Why prepare before the sky turns green

North Texas gets its most severe weather in the spring, when warm, humid air meets cooler air and builds thunderstorms with heavy rain, hail and wind. Forney sits in that pattern like the rest of the Dallas area. Storms often arrive with little notice and can drop a lot of water in a short time.

Most storm water losses start small. A clogged gutter spills at the foundation, a cracked seal lets rain into a wall or a low spot in the yard slopes toward the garage. Fixing those weak points before the season costs far less than cleaning up after a loss.

Roof, gutters and the first line of defense

The roof is where most storm leaks start. Walk the yard with binoculars and look for missing shingles, curled edges and bright patches where granules have worn away. Pay attention to flashing around chimneys, vents and skylights, because those joints open up over time.

Gutters and downspouts carry water away from the house, but only when they are clear. Leaves and shingle grit collect in gutters, and a full gutter dumps water along the foundation. Make sure downspouts discharge several feet from the wall so water does not pool against it. The checklist works the same way for a lot in Seagoville.

Drainage and grading around the lot

Look at your yard right after a hard rain. Where does the water go and where does it stand? Low spots near the foundation, patios that slope toward the house and flower beds that trap water all push moisture against the walls.

Add soil to build a slight slope away from the house, and keep flower bed edging from forming a dam. Garage door thresholds deserve a close look as well, since a worn seal lets water roll straight inside. If you are in Mesquite or another neighboring city, the same checks apply to your lot.

Inside the house before the storm arrives

A few quick moves protect belongings when heavy rain is in the forecast.

  • Move boxes, stored furniture and electronics off the garage floor and onto shelves.
  • Know where your main water shutoff valve and electrical panel are and how to use them.
  • Unplug sensitive electronics that sit near windows or low outlets.
  • Photograph each room and the exterior now, so you have a before record for any claim.
  • Keep a flashlight, a phone charger and a basic tool kit where you can reach them.

If water does enter, safety comes first. Keep away from standing water if outlets or appliances are submerged.

Alerts, plans and who to call

Sign up for local alerts and keep wireless emergency alerts turned on in your phone. The National Weather Service forecast office serving the Dallas and Fort Worth area issues watches and warnings for the region, including Kaufman County. A watch means conditions are favorable, and a warning means severe weather is happening or about to.

Decide ahead of time where the household will shelter and how you will check on neighbors. Keep your insurance agent's number and a restoration company's number in your phone. That way you are not searching for them when the power is out.

After the storm: first steps if water got in

Wait until it is safe to go outside, then look for the water's entry point. Take photos before you move anything. If standing water is deep enough to reach outlets, stay out and call for help, since electrical hazards are real in a flooded space.

Then begin water extraction to remove standing water and open wet areas for drying. Quick action keeps water out of the walls and subfloor, which is where most long-term damage begins. Document what you see, and report the loss to your insurer.

Hail and wind: what to do with roof damage

Hail leaves its marks on shingles, gutters, siding and vents. After a storm, look for dents in soft metal such as downspouts and vent caps, which hint at roof damage. Bruised shingles can leak for months before a stain appears inside.

If you find damage, photograph it and call your insurer. Cover exposed areas with a tarp to prevent more rain from entering, and keep the receipt. Homeowners in Mesquite and Forney both see these patterns in spring.

Why quick drying matters after a storm

When storm water gets in, the clock starts. Wet drywall, insulation and flooring begin to deteriorate and can grow mold if they stay damp. The sooner standing water is removed and the structure is dried, the less needs to be torn out.

A small amount of water that is dried promptly can often be handled with limited removal, while the same water left for days can spread into walls. Photograph first, then act. Early work is usually the best way to keep costs under control.

Local note: weather and flood sources

The National Weather Service publishes the watches and warnings for North Texas and explains the difference between them. FEMA's Flood Map Service Center lets you check whether your address is in a mapped flood zone, and the Texas Department of Insurance explains that standard homeowners policies exclude flood.

The City of Forney also posts notices about storm drainage and road conditions on its official channels, which are worth following during a heavy storm.
