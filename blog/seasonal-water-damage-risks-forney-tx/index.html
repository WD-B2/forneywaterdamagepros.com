Seasonal Water Damage Risks in Forney, TX

Forney has four water damage seasons, and each one has its own weak points. Here is what to watch and when.

Call (844) 950-6900

Written by the Forney Water Damage Pros team. Published November 13, 2026.

Why the season matters

Water damage is not random. Different times of year stress different parts of a house, and North Texas swings between wet springs, hot summers and occasional hard freezes. Knowing which part is under strain lets you check the right things at the right time.

This guide walks through the calendar and points to the usual failure points. It is general guidance, not a promise about any one house, so use it as a prompt for your own seasonal walkthrough.

Spring: storms and runoff

Heavy thunderstorms bring most of the spring risk. Roofs lose shingles, windows leak under wind-driven rain and water runs toward low points, including garages. After each storm, walk the exterior and look for missing shingles, pooled water and overflowing gutters.

Inside, check ceilings and the corners of rooms for new stains. Early signs are easy to miss, and a small leak can feed the wall cavity for weeks. If you find standing water after a storm, our storm water damage cleanup page explains how the work is handled. Mesquite homeowners see the same pattern through the year.

Summer: the air conditioner works overtime

Forney summers run long and hot, so the air conditioner runs for months. As it cools the air, it removes moisture that drains away through a condensate line. When that line clogs or the drain pan rusts through, water spills onto ceilings, closets and attic floors.

Have the system serviced before the hot season and check the drain line yourself every few weeks. A tripped float switch, a wet spot below the air handler or water on the secondary pan are signs to act on. Our guide to AC leak water damage covers what to do when the water has already spread.

Late summer and the soil

Many North Texas soils are clay-rich, and clay swells when wet and shrinks when dry. A long dry spell can pull soil away from a foundation and shift the ground slightly. Pipes under or near a slab can strain as the ground moves.

Watch for new cracks in drywall or brick, doors that stick and floors that feel uneven. Those signs do not prove a leak, but they are reasons to look closer. A sudden rise in your water bill with no change in use is another clue.

Fall: gutters and drains

Leaves and debris fill gutters in the fall, and the first heavy rain turns that clog into overflow. Water spills along the roofline and soaks the soffit or runs down the siding. Clean gutters once the leaves drop and flush the downspouts with a hose.

Fall is also a good time to check window seals and caulk before cold weather. Moisture that sneaks around a loose window can rot trim over time. A tidy exterior in October prevents damp surprises in February.

Winter: freezes are rare but costly

Hard freezes do not come every year in North Texas, but when they do, the damage can be widespread. The February 2021 winter storm caused pipe failures across the state, and many homes without winter protection lost water service and suffered burst lines. Because freezes are infrequent, many homeowners are not ready for them.

Insulate exposed pipes, disconnect hoses and cover outdoor faucets before a freeze. Know where your main shutoff is so you can close it at the first sign of a split. If a pipe does burst, our frozen pipe water damage page explains what to do and what to expect.

A simple four-visit walkthrough

Put these on your calendar once a year.

  1. Early spring

    Roof, gutters, grading and garage door seal.

  2. Early summer

    AC service, drain line check and attic inspection.

  3. Early fall

    Clean gutters, check windows and test the main shutoff valve.

  4. Before the first freeze

    Wrap exposed pipes, disconnect hoses and know your shutoff.

Humidity and the shoulder seasons

Spring and fall bring swings between warm days and cool nights. When a warm, humid house meets a cool surface, moisture condenses on windows, in attics and on cold pipes. Over weeks, that dampness shows up as peeling paint and dark spots.

Run bath fans, use the range hood while cooking and keep vents unblocked. A basic hygrometer helps you see whether indoor humidity is holding in a comfortable range. Small habits reduce the damp conditions that mold needs.

After a storm or a freeze: what to do first

Whatever the season, the first steps are the same. Stop the source if you can, stay away from electricity near water and photograph the damage. Then call for extraction and drying as soon as the area is safe.

Quick action matters in every season because water travels. It moves along framing, under flooring and into insulation, and it reaches places you cannot see. A crew with moisture meters can find and dry it, and callers in Terrell and Forney reach the same 24/7 line.

Insurance timing by season

Review your policy before the busy seasons. Spring storms often bring wind and hail deductibles that differ from the standard deductible. Winter freezes lead to many burst pipe claims in a short period, which can slow adjusters.

Take photos of your home when it is in good condition. A dated set of images from each season helps document what changed, and it makes a claim easier to support. Keep your agent's contact information handy.

Small habits that cover every season

A few habits pay off all year. Look under sinks and around the water heater when you do other chores, watch your water bill for unexplained jumps and fix drips as soon as you see them. Keep a short list of the tasks above and tick them off each season.

If you do find a leak, act the same day. Small leaks are inexpensive to dry and repair, while the same leak left for months turns into a larger project.

Local note: climate sources

The National Weather Service and NOAA publish climate normals for the Dallas and Fort Worth area, which show the spring rainfall peak and the hot summer pattern. The Texas State Historical Association records the February 2021 winter storm and its effects across the state.

Those sources describe the whole region, so Forney homeowners in Terrell and nearby towns should expect the same general pattern.
