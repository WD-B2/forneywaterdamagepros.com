Mold Removal Services in Forney, TX: How to Hire Well

Texas has specific rules for mold work. Knowing them helps you spot a company that follows the process and one that does not.

Call (844) 950-6900

Written by the Forney Water Damage Pros team. Published November 13, 2026.

What mold removal services actually include

Mold removal services cover more than scrubbing. A complete job finds and fixes the moisture source, contains the work area, removes or cleans affected materials and confirms that the area is clean. Skipping the first step is the most common reason mold returns.

Because the work disturbs spores, containment and air control are part of the job. A company should be able to describe how it keeps affected air from reaching the rest of your home. Our overview of mold remediation explains the stages in more detail.

How Texas licensing is organized

Texas separates the people who test from the people who clean. A licensed mold assessor inspects, writes the remediation protocol and later checks the result. A licensed mold remediation contractor carries out the work. The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation oversees both licenses under Chapter 1958 of the Occupations Code.

The same person or company generally cannot assess and remediate the same project. That rule protects you from a company that finds a problem and also profits from fixing it. When someone offers to do both, ask how they handle that conflict. The same questions apply when hiring a company in Seagoville.

What a written protocol should cover

A protocol is a plan for the job. It should say which areas are affected, what materials will be removed or cleaned, how the area will be contained and what the clearance standard will be. It should also address the moisture source so the result lasts.

If the protocol is vague, you cannot hold anyone to it. Ask for a copy before work starts and read it. A good company is comfortable explaining each step in plain language, and the homeowner should not feel hurried into signing.

Red flags when comparing companies

Be cautious of anyone who quotes a price over the phone without seeing the area, who cannot name the license they hold or who promises that mold will never come back. Mold returns if moisture returns, so no honest company can make that promise. Pressure to sign the same day is another warning sign.

Also be wary of a vague scope such as treat the whole house. Good estimates list the rooms, the materials and the steps. If two bids differ widely, compare what each one includes before you compare totals.

How to compare bids

A simple routine keeps the comparison fair.

  1. Check the license

    Look up the company on the state's license search and confirm the license type matches the work.

  2. Get the scope in writing

    Ask for rooms, materials, containment and clearance to be listed.

  3. Ask about the moisture source

    A bid that ignores the cause is incomplete.

  4. Confirm insurance

    Ask for proof that the company is insured and read the dates.

  5. Compare totals last

    Match scopes first, then compare the numbers.

When water damage and mold come together

Many mold jobs start as a water loss. If a leak went unnoticed, drying and mold work may be needed together, and the order matters. Wet materials are dried or removed first, and mold work follows once the area is stable. A company that handles structural drying can coordinate the two stages.

Ask whether your insurance may cover any part of the work. Some policies limit mold payments, so check before you commit. Ask the company how its records can support any insurance claim you file.

What changes the price of mold removal

Mold jobs vary too much for a single number, so here are the factors that move the total.

  • Size of the affected areaSquare footage and how many rooms are involved drive labor and containment.
  • Materials that must be removedDrywall, insulation and flooring add tear-out, disposal and rebuild.
  • Containment and air controlSealing the work area and filtering the air are part of a proper job.
  • Moisture source repairFixing the leak or humidity problem is separate from the mold work.
  • Assessment and clearanceTesting and the final check are billed by the assessor, not the remediator.

Ask each company to list what is included, then compare the lists before you compare the totals.

What a good first visit looks like

A thorough first visit takes time. The visitor looks at the visible growth, checks for moisture with a meter, asks how the problem started and reads the room for likely hidden spots. You should leave with a clear idea of what is known, what is not and what the next step is.

Be careful with anyone who diagnoses the entire house in a few minutes. Hidden mold is found with tools and careful inspection, not guesswork. A company that explains its limits is usually easier to trust than one that offers certainty.

Questions to ask before you sign

Bring a short list to the first meeting. Ask who holds the Texas license for the work, who writes the protocol, how the area will be contained and how the moisture source will be fixed. Ask what happens if the clearance check does not pass.

Also ask who will be on site each day and whether subcontractors are involved. A clear answer to each question is a good sign. Hesitation or vague replies are worth noting, and you can always take a day to think before committing.

How long the process takes

Timelines depend on size and on how fast the area is dried. A small, contained job may take a few days of work plus time for the assessor to check the result. Larger projects with rebuilding take longer, and drying time is often the part nobody can rush.

Ask for a schedule that separates the stages. That way you know when the containment goes up, when materials come out, when the clearance check happens and when the rebuild can start.

Local note: verifying a license in Texas

The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation maintains a public license search for mold assessors and remediators. Chapter 1958 of the Occupations Code and the department's rules set out when a license is required and how the protocol and clearance steps work. Checking takes a few minutes and is worth doing for any job over the small-area threshold.
