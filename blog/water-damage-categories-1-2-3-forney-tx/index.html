Water Damage Categories 1, 2 and 3 Explained for Forney, TX Homeowners

Not all water is the same. The category tells you how risky it is and how the cleanup has to be done.

Call (844) 950-6900

Written by the Forney Water Damage Pros team. Published November 13, 2026.

Why categories exist

Restoration professionals sort water into three categories based on how contaminated it is. The categories come from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which guides the industry. They tell a crew what protective gear to wear, which materials can be saved and how to dispose of what cannot.

As a homeowner, you do not need to memorize the standard, but understanding the categories helps you ask better questions. It also explains why two jobs of the same size can have very different scopes.

The three categories at a glance

The source of the water is what places it in a category.

CategorySource examplesUsual handling
Category 1Supply line, faucet, sink overflow with clean waterExtract and dry, many materials can be saved.
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow with urine onlyExtract, clean and dry, porous materials often removed.
Category 3Sewage, rising floodwater, stagnant standing waterProtective gear, removal of porous materials, sanitizing.

Category 1: clean water

Category 1 water comes from a sanitary source, like a broken supply line, an overflowing sink with clean water or a leaking water heater. It does not pose a contamination concern at the moment it escapes. Most materials can be dried and saved if the response is quick.

Clean water does not stay clean forever. It picks up contaminants as it moves through a building and sits in materials. This is why speed matters even with the cleanest water.

Category 2: significantly contaminated water

Category 2 water, sometimes called gray water, contains contaminants that could cause discomfort if exposed or ingested. Sources include a washing machine overflow, a dishwasher discharge or a toilet overflow without feces. Porous materials such as carpet pad and drywall are often removed because they absorb the contamination.

Hard surfaces can be cleaned and disinfected. The crew uses protective equipment and treats the water with more care than clean water. The decision about which materials to keep depends on what the water touched and how long it sat.

Category 3: grossly contaminated water

Category 3 water is grossly contaminated and may carry harmful agents. Sewage backups, rising floodwater from rivers or streams and standing water that has sat for a long time all fall into this category. Crews wear protective gear and contain the area.

Porous materials that touched Category 3 water are removed and discarded. Hard materials are cleaned and treated. Our sewage backup cleanup page walks through the process, and the same rules apply to outside water that comes in during a storm, as explained on the flood damage restoration page.

How water can change categories

Water can move down the scale as time passes. Clean water from a broken pipe that sits for two or three days in warm conditions can degrade into a higher category. Bacteria multiply in warm, wet materials, and organic debris adds to the mix.

That is why a long-delayed response can change the plan. A job that would have been a simple dry-out on day one may need more removal on day four. In Forney summers, warmth speeds that change.

Classes: how much water is in the building

Category is about contamination, and class is about how much and how deep the water went.

ClassDescriptionExample
Class 1Small area, low absorptionA small leak on a hard floor.
Class 2Larger area, carpet and cushion affectedA room with wet carpet and lower wall.
Class 3Water from above, walls and ceiling saturatedA ceiling leak that drenched the room.
Class 4Deep, low-porosity materials like hardwood, plaster or concreteSlab or hardwood saturation.

What this means for your decisions

Knowing the category lets you decide what to do before the crew arrives. With Category 3 water, stay out of the area and keep children and pets away. With clean water, you can start moving belongings and photographing the damage.

It also helps you understand an estimate. A higher category means more labor, more protective measures and more materials removed. If you are in Terrell or Forney and are not sure what category applies, a quick call with a description of the source will help.

Protecting yourself while you wait for help

If you suspect Category 2 or 3 water, keep people and pets out of the area. Avoid touching the water, and wash your hands well if you do. Do not use a household vacuum to clean it up, and do not walk through it with bare feet.

Turn off power to the affected area if you can do so from a dry spot. Open windows if it is safe, and take photos from the doorway. Then call for help and describe what you see.

Categories and your insurance claim

Insurers pay attention to the category. Some policies treat sewer backup and flood differently from a burst pipe, and each can involve separate coverage or limits. Reporting the source accurately helps the adjuster handle your claim.

Ask your restoration company to note the category and class in its paperwork. That record explains why certain materials were removed and supports the scope in your claim.

Why the first description matters

When you call, describe the source as clearly as you can. Say where the water came from, what it touched and how long it has been there. Mention anything unusual, such as a smell or color.

That description lets the crew prepare the right gear and plan the first visit. It also gives you a record of what you reported. In Forney and the nearby towns of Seagoville and Terrell, the same questions apply.

Local note: the standard behind the terms

The ANSI/IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration defines the three categories and four classes used on this page. The Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification publishes it, and restoration companies follow it as the industry reference.

Insurers and adjusters also use these terms, so knowing them helps when you read an estimate or talk to a claims representative.
