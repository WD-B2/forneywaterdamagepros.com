Basement flooding emergency: stay out until the power is safe

Water in a basement or other below-grade room is an emergency because it can be deep, it often reaches outlets and equipment, and it keeps coming if the cause is outside. Our 24/7 line is open.

Do not go down while water is standing and the power is on. If the panel is dry and you can reach it safely, turn off power to the lower level, otherwise call the utility.

Basement water damage cleanup is the pumping out of water, removal of wet materials and drying of walls, floors and framing below grade. A below-grade room is usually concrete on three sides, which dries slowly, and its finished walls and floors hide moisture.

Not every Forney home has a basement, but some do

Most homes in this area are built on slabs, and we make no claim about how many have basements. If you have a basement or another below-grade room, the water usually arrives through one of four routes. Groundwater can seep through walls or floors, and surface runoff can enter through a window well or door. A sump or drain can fail, or a plumbing line can leak.

The Texas State Historical Association puts Kaufman County rainfall at about 39 inches a year on clayey subsoils. Clay drains slowly, so water can sit against a foundation wall after heavy rain and push through cracks. The same slow drainage is why a below-grade room can stay damp long after the weather clears.

How basement water is removed

The sequence protects people and the structure.

  1. Make it safe

    Power is cut or confirmed safe, and the water source is identified.

  2. Pump out in stages

    Water is removed gradually so the walls and floor are not stressed by pressure differences.

  3. Remove wet materials

    Carpet, pad, drywall and insulation that cannot be dried are removed.

  4. Clean and sanitize

    Floors and lower walls are cleaned, especially after flood or sewage water.

  5. Dry

    Dehumidifiers and air movers dry concrete, framing and what remains.

After the water is out

Basements dry slowly, so watch for these.

  • A musty smell that returns.
  • Dark spots on walls or the bottom of drywall.
  • Efflorescence, a white powder on concrete.
  • Carpet that feels damp on top of concrete.
  • A dehumidifier that fills quickly every day.

If you see dark growth, see mold remediation. If the water was sewage, see the sewage backup page.

Keeping the next storm out of a lower level

Water that comes from outside is easier to prevent than to clean. Keep gutters clear and make sure downspouts discharge several feet from the foundation. Look at the grade next to the house, because soil should slope away, not toward it. Check window wells for debris and make sure covers fit.

If you have a sump pump, test it before the rainy season by pouring water into the pit, and consider a battery backup because storms often bring power outages. A floor drain that backs up needs a plumber's attention. A few simple checks each spring cost far less than the cleanup after a flood.
